## What This Course Is

This is not a theory course. Every module is grounded in a real codebase — the paulkiat.io platform itself — built using the RPI method in live mob programming sessions. You'll read actual plan documents, see how estimates collapsed from 50 hours to 4, and understand *why* that happened.

The RPI method has three phases:

1. **Research** — Understand the problem space before touching a keyboard. Read the existing code. Map constraints. Study what's already been built. Sit with the question longer than feels comfortable.
2. **Plan** — Write a design document *before* writing code. Define success criteria. Identify risks. Lock in names and interfaces. The plan is a contract you make with your future self.
3. **Implement** — Build demo-first. Small, focused files. Test as you go. Each session ends with something demoable. Nothing more than 500 lines. Never more complexity than needed.

What makes this method powerful is the order. Most teams start with Implement. Some teams remember to Plan. Almost nobody does Research properly. This course fixes that.

## The Faculty

### Lex Fridman — Module 1: Research
*AI researcher, podcast host, MIT lecturer*

Lex brings first-principles thinking to the Research phase. His work at MIT and his long-form conversations with the world's greatest minds have given him a rare skill: the ability to sit with a problem until he truly understands it, rather than jumping to what *looks* like an answer. He asks questions the way a physicist probes axioms — stripping away assumptions until something true remains.

**His lens on RPI**: "The Research phase is where intellectual honesty lives. Most people skip it because impatience feels like momentum. It isn't. Impatience is the enemy of understanding, and misunderstanding is where all the expensive mistakes are born."

---

### Andrew Ng — Module 2: Plan
*Stanford Professor, founder of Coursera, deeplearning.ai, Google Brain co-founder*

Andrew has taught more people machine learning than anyone alive. His gift is structure — taking something complex and breaking it into a sequence that any determined learner can climb. He applies that same pedagogical rigor to the Planning phase: if you can't explain your plan clearly, step by step, you don't have a plan. You have a wish.

**His lens on RPI**: "Planning is teaching yourself what you're going to build before you build it. A good plan is a curriculum. It has prerequisites, milestones, and clear success criteria. If you wouldn't trust a course with no syllabus, don't trust a feature with no plan."

---

### Woody Zuill — Module 3: Implement
*Creator of Mob Programming, Whole Team Software development pioneer*

Woody originated the practice of Mob Programming — the whole team, working on one thing, together, all day. He's spent decades watching teams work, noticing what helps and what hurts, and developing the "Turn Up the Good" philosophy: instead of trying to fix what's broken, amplify what's working. His approach to Implementation is humble, reflective, and deeply human.

**His lens on RPI**: "I've noticed that when we implement together — the whole team, one screen — we stop hiding our confusion. And that's when the real work begins. Implementation isn't the end of thinking. It's thinking made visible."

---

### Jensen Huang — Module 4: Acceleration
*CEO of NVIDIA, architect of the AI computing era*

Jensen thinks in systems. He took a graphics chip company and turned it into the infrastructure of the AI revolution. His insight — that the right investment in computing infrastructure pays geometric dividends — applies equally to development methodology. With AI in the loop, RPI doesn't just become faster. It becomes a different kind of process entirely.

**His lens on RPI**: "This is the moment where the compounding begins. When you put AI inside the RPI loop — not as a crutch, but as an accelerant — you're not just saving time. You're expanding what's possible in a single session. The teams that understand this will build ten years of product in three."
